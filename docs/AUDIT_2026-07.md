# Production-readiness audit — July 2026

*What was checked, what was wrong, what was changed, and what is still open.
Written for whoever maintains the Atlas next; no prior context assumed.*

Audit date: 2026-07-26/27 · Baseline commit: `b390085` · Result commit: `346e557`

---

## 1. How the audit was run

Fourteen independent reviews covered the whole system: the two main datasets, the
companion data files, the validator itself, all five ETL pipelines, both halves of
`ask-engine.js`, all four pages, the Cloudflare worker, the three GitHub Actions
workflows, every documentation file, provenance end-to-end, and security.

Every finding then went to a separate reviewer whose instructions were to **refute
it** — to prove it exaggerated, intentional, or already handled elsewhere. Only
findings that survived that pass were treated as real.

**137 raised → 92 confirmed → 6 refuted** (the rest were low-severity polish). Of
the 92 confirmed, **86 are fixed**; 6 were deliberately not fixed (§5).

The audit's own numbers were then re-verified independently: 60 live World Bank API
comparisons across 10 countries found **zero mismatches**, confirming the World Bank
pipeline reproduces its source exactly.

---

## 2. The five that mattered most

Each of these produced confident, wrong, or stale output rather than an error — the
failure mode this product can least afford.

### 2.1 "Rising this week" was, for two-thirds of topics, three weeks old

`series_stats()` read *the last 7 positions in an array* as *the last 7 days*. That
holds only while every series ends on the same day. It did not: the daily Wikipedia
fetch had been hitting its 85-minute cap since ~2026-07-05, and because both fetch
steps were marked `continue-on-error`, **the workflow reported success every single
day**. The checkpoint logic then re-published ~2,000 frozen series, and the Topic
Explorer displayed their three-week-old numbers under "updated today".

*Fixed three ways.* Velocity windows are anchored by calendar date to the file's own
`measured_as_of`; a series with no data in that window is excluded rather than
averaged in. The fetcher now processes **stalest series first**, so whatever a
timeout cuts off is exactly what runs first next time — a truncated run is
self-healing. And the workflow now **fails** when most series are stale, so the
alert fires instead of a green tick.

*Visible effect:* the Topic Explorer went from claiming 149 tracked topics to
"89 topics measured … 70 more have no current data and are not scored today". That
is a real reduction in what the page claims — and the truth.

### 2.2 The press-freedom index was corrupt, and hand-typed

`RSF_RANK_2025` was 174 countries transcribed by hand. Checked against RSF's own
"previous edition" column: **45 ranks disagreed with RSF's record**, and Eritrea was
ranked **181 in a 180-country index**.

*Fixed by removing the hand-typing.* `scripts/fetch_rsf.py` downloads RSF's published
CSV and writes `data/sources/rsf/rsf_index.json`, with validation that refuses to
overwrite good data with a bad fetch. The 2026 edition is integrated: 180 countries,
plus RSF's five sub-indicators and prior-year ranks that were never available before.
Haiti gained coverage; 162 ranks updated. Next year's refresh is one command.

### 2.3 The map ignored 64 countries

Clicking Afghanistan, DR Congo, North Korea, the UAE or 60 others did nothing at all.
The click handler only opened the "covered" tier — but those countries have full
profiles, appear in the sidebar with a **PROFILE** button, and are labelled
"Profile only" in the map's own legend. Verified live before and after.

### 2.4 Refreshed data published with no validation

`validate_atlas.py` — the guard written after the June-2026 fabrication incident —
**was never run by any workflow**. The weekly refresh committed and published
whatever the APIs returned. It is now a publication gate: validation failure stops
the commit, the site keeps serving the last good data, and an Issue opens.

Separately, both failure-alert steps used a GitHub label that did not exist, so
`gh issue create` exited non-zero and **the alert itself failed silently** — exactly
what had been happening to the source watchdog since it shipped. Labels created;
alerts now fall back to filing unlabelled rather than losing the alarm.

### 2.5 Ordinary English words resolved to countries

- *"Which platform for a **car** safety campaign?"* → a full strategic brief on the
  **Central African Republic** (`car` was an alias for CAF).
- *"How do we reach **male** audiences in Brazil?"* → Brazil **and the Maldives**
  (Malé, the capital, normalises to "male").
- *"How do we support **front line** workers?"* → the LINE messaging app.
- *"What is the radio **reach** in Kenya?"* → a 5,500-character consulting brief
  instead of the one-line figure asked for.

All four now behave correctly; the car question asks *which region* rather than
guessing.

## 3. Everything else that was fixed

**Data & provenance.** Ten countries published a literacy figure whose citation a
2026-07-01 regression had dropped — and because the ETL only re-attached a source
"if one already existed", it could never come back. Citations are now reconstructed
from the indicator table, and every World Bank value carries its own observation
year. Burundi's capital (Gitega), Gabon's form of government, and 22 countries'
media notes (raw `&eacute;` HTML entities rendering as garbage in briefs) corrected.
`population_year` now means population's own year rather than the newest year across
all 15 indicators.

**Metric honesty.** GDELT's per-country figure was published as `coverage_share_pct`
— "share of coverage". It is Volume Intensity: the share of *that country's own*
news output matching the topic, so a small market covering something obsessively
outranks a large one publishing far more about it. Renamed to
`media_intensity_by_country` / `pct_of_country_news_volume` and re-described at every
surface. Stale GDELT volume is now dropped rather than presented as a current
7-day count, and the partial current day is excluded.

**Regions.** `europe`, `asia`, `balkans`, `scandinavia` and `gulf` resolved through
the World Bank's region codes, which file Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia and the five
Central Asian republics under "Europe". Balkans answers led with Spain, Portugal and
Malta; Gulf answers included Türkiye, Iraq, Syria and Yemen. All now use explicit
country lists, with `central europe` and the missing adjective forms added.

**Validator.** Now materially stricter: per-survey country rosters (a real source
label borrowed for a country that survey never visited is the same trick in better
disguise), duplicate-key detection, range checks on every headline figure, citation
presence as an error rather than a warning, and a roster assertion so a country
cannot quietly vanish.

**Interface.** Every Market Finder form label failed WCAG AA at 2.6:1 — colours are
now standardised across all four pages and pass at every size. Country search could
not find Türkiye by any spelling. The Sources tab rendered non-URL citation text
(Freedom House's "official data files provided by…") as a dead clickable link. When
`countries.json` failed to load, the page silently fell back to a 10-country
snapshot with no notice. `topics.html` had no HTML escaping at all; `index.html` had
none either. Profile tabs had `role="tab"` but no ARIA wiring. Market Finder's
"Profile ↗" links promised a country profile and delivered the bare map — deep
links (`index.html?country=KEN`) now exist.

**Worker.** `worker/analyst-worker.js` had drifted far behind the engine it mirrors
and would, if deployed, have silently regressed the analyst to a superseded format.
It is now switched off in its own code and clearly marked experimental in its header,
its deploy guide, the README and `ask.html`.

**Documentation.** Three root-level files duplicated `docs/` versions — and the root
copy of `DATA_SCHEMA.md` was the stale one, missing the "historical document"
warning. Deleted. `DEMO_SCRIPT.md` walked a presenter through brief sections the
engine stopped producing in July. `DATA_SOURCES.md` listed four integrated surveys
as still pending registration. `PLAN.md` was April's superseded roadmap. The eval
README documented two of three suites. `AUTOMATION.md` gained a troubleshooting
section written for a non-coder.

## 3a. Citation standard (added after the audit)

One dimension of the audit — provenance and citations — had its verification stage
cut short by a usage limit, so its findings were triaged separately afterwards.
Three were already fixed incidentally (the smartphone mis-attribution, the ten
uncited literacy figures, the stale source registry). The rest produced these
changes:

**Every figure the site displays now has a source.** The leading-outlet lists
(`top_tv`, `top_radio`, `top_online_news`, `top_social`) had no citation anywhere,
while the Sources tab promised that every data point traced to a primary source.
They are an editorial compilation, not a measurement, and now say exactly that in
all 195 records — and the promise itself was reworded in all six languages to match
what the page can actually deliver. The analyst's platform-footprint footnote used
to tell readers to "see each country's profile for its citation" for a citation
that did not exist; it now describes the real provenance and warns that the lists
are an ordering, not audience share.

**The standard, stated deliberately:** the three answer surfaces — the AI Analyst (named "Ask the Analyst" at audit time),
Market Finder and Topic Explorer — use a numbered **Sources** footnote block in the
same visual style. The country profile uses its dedicated **Sources tab**, which is
a superset: one entry per field, with the retrieval year. Market Finder had no
citation apparatus at all before this and now lists, by name, the surveys actually
used in the result set plus the World Bank, CLDR, RSF and Freedom House series
behind its other columns.

Numbered footnotes were deliberately *not* forced onto the country profile. That
panel shows 40+ fields; a footnote marker on each, resolving to a list, would be
worse to read than the per-field Sources tab it already has. Consistency here means
every surface has one clearly labelled sources block in a predictable place — not
that a 40-field reference panel imitates a prose answer.

**Licences are now stated on the site.** The Sources/Methodology modal names the
terms the Atlas redistributes under — CC BY 4.0 for World Bank data, Unicode
License v3 for CLDR, CC0 for Wikimedia Pageviews, public domain for the CIA World
Factbook, attribution-based research reuse for the barometer programmes — in all
six languages.

## 6. Known limitations

These are honest constraints, not defects. Several are consequences of the $0 rule.

1. **Survey coverage is 126 of 195 countries.** The other 69 have no free
   nationally-representative media survey. They are shown as "profile only",
   excluded from Market Finder *by name with a reason*, and never estimated.
2. **Constructs are not comparable across sources.** Reuters DNR measures weekly
   reach; Arab and Asian Barometer measure a single "most important channel"; WVS
   measures daily-or-weekly. The engine labels every one, but a reader comparing a
   DNR country to a Barometer country is comparing different questions.
3. **Country attribution of topic trends is a documented approximation.** Wikipedia
   pageviews are per *language edition*, mapped to countries by speaker-population
   weights. It is labelled as an approximation everywhere it appears.
4. **The trend fetch is rate-limit-bound.** Wikimedia throttles shared CI IPs hard.
   Coverage should recover over consecutive runs now that the fetcher is
   stalest-first and failures are visible, but it may not reach 100% every day.
   Watch `coverage.series_stale_excluded` in `topic_intelligence.json`.
5. **Survey microdata is not in the repo.** Licences require individual registration,
   so `compute_*.py` scripts depend on files in a local `~/Downloads`. The scripts
   document exactly which file and where to get it, but a successor cannot re-run
   them without downloading the data themselves.
6. **`static_countries.json` is hand-maintained and unrefreshed.** Capitals,
   governments and currencies go stale with political events. Two entries still
   labelled "Transitional government" (Chad, Syria) need a human decision. Re-read
   these annually — the file now says so at the top.
7. **One CDN dependency remains.** The map's boundary file is fetched from jsDelivr.
   It is now pinned to an immutable commit rather than a moving branch, but the map
   still needs that host to be reachable.
8. **The worker is unfinished.** Left switched off and clearly marked. If anyone
   re-enables it, it needs its own invariant suite first — the eval suites cover
   only the browser engine.

## 7. Recommended next steps

In priority order for the remaining runway:

1. **Watch the trend engine for a week.** The staleness alarm is new. If
   `series_stale_excluded` is not falling, raise the Wikipedia step's
   `timeout-minutes` or `DORMANT_MEAN` (both documented in `AUTOMATION.md`).
2. **Resolve the two "Transitional government" entries** (Chad, Syria) against the
   CIA World Factbook — a 10-minute human check.
3. **Do the same annual read-through for capitals and currencies.**
4. **Leave the worker off.** Nothing needs it, and it is the largest untested surface.
5. **Consider a smaller boundary file.** The map's GeoJSON is 4.3 MB of a ~5.7 MB
   page load. A lower-resolution file would roughly halve load time at some cost to
   coastline detail — a visual-quality decision, not a technical one.

## 8. Production readiness

**The Atlas is fit to deploy and present in a UN setting.**

Every automated gate passes: the validator reports 0 errors and 0 warnings across
195 countries; the golden-100 acceptance test answers 98 and asks for clarification
twice with no crashes and no generic refusals; the strategy suite is 18/18; the
Market Finder invariant suite is 73/73. All four pages were driven live in a browser
on desktop and mobile — no console errors, no layout overflow, no broken links, and
no unescaped-input execution across the injection attempts tried.

The qualification worth stating plainly: **the Atlas is now honest about being
smaller than it looked.** The Topic Explorer reports 89 measurable topics instead of
149; Market Finder names the countries it cannot rank; the analyst declines
questions it used to answer confidently and wrongly. That is the intended direction
for an evidence-first product, but a supervisor who saw the July version will notice
that some numbers went down. They went down because they were wrong.

What carries the most residual risk is not code — it is §6.5 and §6.6: the survey
pipelines that cannot be re-run without re-downloading licensed microdata, and the
hand-maintained country file that nothing refreshes. Both are documented; neither
has an automated solution at $0.
